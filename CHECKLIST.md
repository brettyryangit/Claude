# The Six Questions

Run these against every trade. A "yes" is not a verdict on the trade's outcome —
it is a flag that the decision came from somewhere other than the plan.

### 1. Do you regularly punt a trade with real money out of sheer boredom due to inactivity?

Boredom is not a signal. Sitting on your hands is a position. If nothing on the
plan triggered, the correct trade was no trade.

### 2. Have you risked more than usual because your last few trades were losers?

Size is set by the plan and the account, never by the emotional balance of the
last three results. Increasing risk to "get it back" converts a normal drawdown
into a real one.

### 3. Have you decided to stop using something because it hasn't worked the last handful of times you used it?

A handful of samples tells you nothing about an edge. Abandoning a method on a
small losing run is how you end up permanently one step behind, arriving at each
approach just as it stops working.

### 4. Has someone saying they have made 40% in a week made you think they were a great trader?

One week is noise, and you never see the size, the risk taken, or the weeks they
didn't post. Judge process and consistency, not screenshots.

### 5. Have you thought about stopping for the day after a winner for fear of giving it all back?

Treating the day's P&L as something to protect means the market is being traded
against the scoreboard rather than the setups. The setups don't know what the
morning did.

### 6. Have you moved a stop to break even for the only reason that you cannot now lose?

"I can't lose now" is a feeling, not an invalidation level. If the trade thesis
hasn't changed, the stop hasn't earned the right to move — and moving it just
converts winners into scratches.
