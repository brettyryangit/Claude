## Post-mortem

**What actually happened:** The short never got going. Rather than sit and wait
for 4,026.20 to decide it, the position came off for a 0.15R scratch because the
5-minute wasn't following through.

**The honest reading:** this isn't a rule break, because there was no rule — the
"How I'll manage it" box was left as TBC, and the trade then had to be managed
anyway. So the decision got made under pressure instead of in advance. That's
the actual finding from trade 0001, and it's a cheap one to learn at $19.80.

Cutting a non-performing trade is a legitimate technique. But undefined, it
can't be evaluated and it can't be repeated — and it has a specific failure mode
worth watching for across the next twenty trades: if the 2.1:1 setups keep
getting cut at −0.15R because they didn't move fast enough, the arithmetic never
gets a chance to pay. Paying the toll ten times to dodge one full stop is worse
than taking the stop.

The thing that decides which of those this was is the counterfactual below.

**What I did well:** Small loss, no averaging down, no widening the stop, no
revenge re-entry. Loss was 15% of what was budgeted. And the instinct to go and
check what the trade actually did afterwards is the right instinct — that's the
data that turns a judgment call into a rule.

**What I'd do differently:** Write the no-follow-through rule down *before* the
next entry, in bars or minutes, so it becomes a plan instead of a feeling.
Something testable, e.g. *"if the trade hasn't moved 1R in my favour within N
5-minute bars, flat"* — the value of N is the open question, and the
counterfactual log is how it gets answered.

**Was this a good trade?** Undecided until the counterfactual is filled in.
Good entry logic and good loss control; undefined exit.
